Quarterly Research Summary: Renewable Energy Adoption

Executive Summary
This report examines the adoption of renewable energy sources across
residential and commercial sectors over the past year. Solar power
installations grew by 34% year over year, driven largely by falling
panel costs and expanded government incentive programs. Wind energy
adoption grew more modestly, at 11%, primarily in coastal and
high-wind-corridor regions.

Key Findings
1. Residential solar adoption is now most concentrated in suburban
   areas with high average sunlight exposure and supportive local
   permitting processes.
2. Battery storage attachment rates rose sharply, with 61% of new
   solar installations in the past quarter including a home battery,
   up from 38% the previous year.
3. Commercial adoption lagged residential adoption, constrained mainly
   by longer approval timelines and higher upfront capital requirements.
4. Wind energy remains geographically concentrated, with over 70% of
   new installations occurring in just four regions.

Challenges
Grid interconnection delays remain the most commonly cited barrier to
faster renewable adoption, followed by financing complexity for
commercial projects and a shortage of certified installers in
several rapidly growing markets.

Recommendations
The report recommends streamlining interconnection approval timelines,
expanding installer certification programs, and extending incentive
programs to commercial properties to close the adoption gap between
residential and commercial sectors.

Conclusion
Overall, renewable energy adoption continued to accelerate this
quarter, with residential solar leading growth. Addressing
interconnection delays and installer shortages would likely unlock
significantly faster adoption across both residential and commercial
markets going forward.
